How to Access Your Ancestry Account and Tree as an Existing Member
==========================================================
For existing Ancestry members, your account and family tree are the central repositories of your genealogical research.

Your account contains your subscription information, personal settings, and all the family trees you have created, while your family tree holds the names, dates, records, and photos you have gathered about your ancestors. Accessing both your account and your tree is essential for continuing your research, managing your membership, and preserving your family history. This comprehensive guide explains everything you need to know about accessing your Ancestry account and tree as an existing member, from the sign-in process to navigating your account dashboard, managing multiple trees, and troubleshooting common access issues.

Understanding Your Ancestry Account and Tree
============================================

Your Ancestry account serves as the container for all your genealogy research. When you sign in, you gain access to your account dashboard, which provides an overview of your membership status, recent activity, and links to your family trees. Your account also stores your personal information, privacy settings, and communication preferences.

Your family tree is the primary tool for organizing your genealogical research. It contains profiles of individuals, relationships between them, attached records, and photographs. Each tree is independent, so you can maintain separate trees for different branches of your family or for different research projects.

Your Ancestry account and tree are tied together: you need to sign in to your account to access your tree, and your tree is stored within your account. This integration ensures that your research is always available when you sign in, whether from a computer, tablet, or smartphone.

Signing In to Your Ancestry Account
===================================

Signing in to your Ancestry account is the first step to accessing both your account settings and your family tree. The sign-in process is designed to be straightforward and secure.

To sign in to your Ancestry account, open your web browser and navigate to the Ancestry website. In the top-right corner of any page, click the "Sign In" button. You will be taken to the sign-in page where you can enter your credentials.

On the sign-in page, enter the email address or username associated with your Ancestry account and your password. If you have enabled two-step verification for added security, you will be prompted to enter a verification code sent to your registered email or phone number. This extra layer of protection helps keep your research safe.

If you cannot remember your password, click the "Forgot password" link on the sign-in page. You will receive an email with instructions to create a new password. If you have reset your password and still cannot sign in, clearing your browser's cache and cookies may help resolve the issue.

Once you have entered your credentials and completed any verification steps, click "Sign In" to access your account dashboard. If you are already signed in on the same browser, the website may redirect you directly to your dashboard.

Navigating Your Account Dashboard
=================================

After signing in, you will arrive at your account dashboard. This page provides an overview of your account and serves as the starting point for accessing your trees and managing your membership.

Your account dashboard displays your profile picture or initials, your name, and your membership status. You can see whether you are a free member or have a paid subscription, and when your subscription renews. This information helps you stay informed about your account status.

From the dashboard, you can access your family trees by clicking on the "Trees" tab in the main navigation menu. You can also view recent activity, such as new hints or messages. The dashboard provides quick links to common tasks, such as searching records, viewing DNA results, or managing your account settings.

If you are a new user or have not yet created a tree, the dashboard may prompt you to start building your tree. If you are an existing member with trees already created, your trees will appear in the "Trees" section, ready for you to open and continue your research.

Accessing Your Family Tree
==========================

Once you have signed in to your account, accessing your family tree is the next step. Your trees are stored in your account and can be opened from the "Trees" page.

To access your family tree, click on the "Trees" tab in the main navigation menu at the top of the page. This will display a list of all the family trees associated with your account. Each tree is listed with its name, the date it was last updated, and the number of people in it.

Click on the name of the tree you wish to access. The tree will open in pedigree view, displaying you at the center with your parents, grandparents, and other direct ancestors extending backward in time. You can switch to other views, such as family view or list view, to explore your tree from different perspectives.

If you have multiple trees, ensure you are selecting the correct one. Each tree is independent, and changes made to one tree do not affect others. If you are unsure which tree contains your research, you can open each one to review its contents.

Understanding Your Family Tree Views
====================================

Ancestry provides multiple views to help you explore your family tree. Understanding these views helps you navigate your tree effectively and see your family history from different perspectives.

Pedigree view is the default when you open your family tree. It shows you at the center, with your direct ancestors extending backward. This view is excellent for seeing your direct lineage at a glance and for navigating between generations quickly.

Family view focuses on an individual and displays their spouse, children, parents, and siblings. This view is ideal for understanding the full family unit and for exploring collateral relatives. You can click on any person in the view to make them the center.

List view displays the people in your tree as a list, with basic information such as name, birth date, and death date. This view is useful for finding specific individuals quickly, especially if you have a large tree. You can sort the list by name, date, or other criteria.

Landscape view provides a broader perspective, allowing you to see multiple generations and branches simultaneously. This view is helpful for understanding the overall structure of your family tree and for identifying gaps in your research.

Managing Your Account Settings
==============================

Your account settings allow you to control your personal information, privacy, and communication preferences. Managing these settings ensures that your Ancestry experience is tailored to your needs.

To access your account settings, click on your profile picture or initials in the top-right corner of the page, then select "Account Settings" from the dropdown menu. Here you can update your personal information, including your name, email address, and password.

In the privacy settings, you can control who can see your profile and how much information is displayed. You can choose to make your profile visible to other members or keep it private. You can also control what information appears in your public profile, such as your research interests.

In the communication preferences, you can choose what types of emails you receive from Ancestry. You can opt in or out of newsletters, hints, and promotional offers. Managing these preferences ensures you receive the information you want without being overwhelmed.

Managing Multiple Family Trees
==============================

If you have multiple family trees in your Ancestry account, understanding how to manage them helps keep your research organized and accessible.

To create a new family tree, navigate to the "Trees" page and click the "Create a new tree" button. You will be prompted to enter a name for your new tree and provide basic information about the starting individual. Once created, your new tree will appear alongside your other trees in the list.

To switch between trees, return to the "Trees" page and click on the name of the tree you wish to access. You can work on multiple trees independently, and changes made to one tree do not affect others.

If you no longer need a tree, you can delete it from the "Trees" page. However, be cautious when deleting a tree, as this action cannot be undone and all information in the tree will be permanently lost. Consider downloading a GEDCOM file of the tree before deleting it if you want to preserve the data.

Sharing Your Family Tree
========================

Ancestry allows you to share your family tree with others, enabling collaboration with relatives and fellow researchers. Sharing can be done at different access levels, depending on how much control you want to give others.

To share your family tree, open the tree you wish to share and click on the "Share" button, which is typically located in the upper-right corner of the tree view. You will be presented with options to invite specific people by email or to generate a sharing link.

When you invite someone by email, you can choose whether they can view or edit the tree. View-only access allows them to see the tree but not make any changes. Edit access allows them to add, edit, and delete information, making them collaborators on your research.

If you choose to generate a sharing link, you can share the link with anyone. You can also set an expiration date for the link and control whether recipients can view or edit the tree. This is useful for sharing your research with a larger audience without sending individual invitations.

Troubleshooting Access Issues
=============================

Even with the correct credentials and a successful sign-in, users may occasionally encounter issues accessing their account or tree. Understanding common problems and their solutions helps resolve these issues quickly.

If you have signed in but cannot see your trees, ensure that you are looking at the correct account. If you have multiple Ancestry accounts, your trees may be stored under a different account. Try signing out and signing back in with any other email addresses you may have used.

If your account settings do not reflect your current membership status, sign out and sign back in to refresh your account information. Clearing your browser's cache and cookies may also help resolve this issue.

If your tree is not appearing in the list, it may have been accidentally deleted or hidden. Check the "Trees" page carefully and look for any archived or hidden trees. If you still cannot find your tree, contact Ancestry support for assistance.
